The method, plainly

Cost per year beats sticker shock. A $1,450 repair that buys 3 years costs about $483 a year for the fix itself. A $6,800 replacement spread over a 15-year life costs about $453 a year — before its lower running cost. That comparison, not the quote's size, decides it.

The 50% rule is a guardrail, not gospel. Repair over half of replacement cost on a unit past half its life is usually throwing good money after old metal. Under it, age and running cost still get a vote.

Old machines tax you quietly. The running-cost gap between an old unit and a new one, multiplied by the years you'll keep paying it, belongs in the repair column — because choosing repair is choosing that tax.
